# Quality Rules

## Douyin Quality Gate

Use this checklist before generating and after generating an episode.

## Must Pass

- Within 0.5 seconds, is there a hard interruption?
- Within 3 seconds, does the sentence skeleton appear?
- Within 3 seconds, is there a Chinese memory formula?
- Does the episode start from a Chinese expression pain point?
- Does it avoid grammar-term-first opening?
- Is there at least one clear scene element?
- Is there a visible A/B slot swap animation?
- Is there at least one drill-repeat section?
- Does a clear visual change happen every 2-3 seconds?
- Does it feel like a short video instead of a PPT?
- Within 10 seconds, does the viewer know what they learned?
- Does the ending leave one strong memory point instead of a full lesson summary?
- Do the late scenes avoid large empty layout areas, especially in compare or summary cards?
- Is the bottom subtitle safe area clear of important UI and card content?
- Are usage/reuse scenes and final lock scenes free of accidental component overlap?
- Does the usage scene avoid looking like a symmetric two-column lesson slide?
- Is there exactly one dominant focal answer in the usage scene?
- Does the ending have a real closing shell instead of cutting off after the explanation?
- If there is large negative space, does it feel intentionally activated rather than unfinished?
- Does the episode feel distinct from neighboring episodes rather than like the same shell with swapped text?
- Is the creator attitude visible, instead of neutral teaching voice?
- Is all important content safely above the platform caption overlap zone?
- If template-level shells, safe-area rules, or scene fixes changed, was the episode rerendered afterward?

## One-Vote Rejection

- It looks like a PPT.
- It looks like a course cover.
- The whole thing is just black background text cards.
- The sentence skeleton appears after 3 seconds.
- There is no slot swap.
- There is no scene feeling.
- The cover has more than 3 layers of information.
- The examples feel like textbook material.
- The voiceover sounds like a grammar teacher.
- The episode explains everything but never drills the memory.
- The usage scene looks like a PowerPoint comparison slide.
- The final shot feels abrupt, emotionally flat, or unfinished.
- Empty space is solved by adding more text instead of atmosphere or scene support.
- It feels like a batch clone of the previous episode.
- The ending card is technically clean but emotionally generic.
- Important English or Chinese text competes with the publish-caption zone.
- The export is stale relative to the current template rules.

## Suggested Still Frames

Render frames around:

- Hook.
- Flash pain points.
- Pattern reveal.
- Slot replacement.
- First example breakdown.
- Drill section.
- Usage scene.
- Final compare / lock scene.
- Final summary.
