# Facts: 002_teen_sleep_news

Every claim with a number, with its source and the quoted wording (checked 2026-10-27).

| On screen | Source | Quoted wording |
|---|---|---|
| Teens aged 13–18 need 8–10 hours of sleep a night | CDC YRBS "Sleep" page (updated Aug 14, 2026), citing the American Academy of Sleep Medicine: https://www.cdc.gov/yrbs/youth-health-in-focus/sleep.html | "The American Academy of Sleep Medicine recommends that adolescents aged 13–18 get 8 to 10 hours of sleep a night." |
| Only about 1 in 4 US high school students get at least 8 hours on school nights (24 %, 2025) | Same page, 2025 Youth Risk Behavior Survey: https://www.cdc.gov/yrbs/youth-health-in-focus/sleep.html | "In 2025, about 1 in 4 high school students got at least 8 hours of sleep on an average school night." Chart: 24 % in 2025. |

Claims without numbers (sleep helps the brain store what was learned; staying up late to study can make memory worse; putting the phone away before bed helps) are general advice, worded with "can", not presented as statistics.

The older, often-quoted "72.7 % of high school students do not get enough sleep" is 2015 data (archive.cdc.gov) and is not used.
